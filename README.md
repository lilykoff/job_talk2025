**Title**: Methods and Applications for High-Frequency Biosignals Data
**Abstract**

Data from wearable devices, such as accelerometers, are increasingly common in both consumer and research settings. These data have the potential to provide rich insights about human movement and health but present computational and methodological challenges. In this talk, I present a series of questions motivated by wearable accelerometry that illustrate the complexity and promise of these data. First, I examine whether individuals can be uniquely identified by their accelerometry-derived walking pattern, in both controlled and free-living settings. Next, I discuss approaches for estimating steps from free-living accelerometry and methods for using these estimates for population-level inference. Finally, I consider how these methods could be applied to other high-frequency hemodynamics data collected during cardiac surgery to extract meaningful clinical insights.
